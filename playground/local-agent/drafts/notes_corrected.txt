Artificial intelligence is a growing and demanding field in computer science. We can see that modern equipment can perform some form of intelligent behavior. So, today’s human society is becoming accustomed to the intelligence embedded in modern machines and devices.

Actually, what is the definition of artificial intelligence? Before we begin to define artificial intelligence, we must first analyze natural intelligence. Natural intelligence is a God-given ability, and only our brains are capable of exhibiting it; in other words, we cannot replicate natural intelligence because it is a God-given trait. Therefore, scientists have been trying to make machines intelligent through artificial intelligence, although machines cannot perfectly emulate human brains.

Interestingly, human brain functions and machine functions differ and, to some extent, are complementary. The human brain can perform tasks that are very difficult to simulate in machines—such as seeing, hearing, and sensing—which are fundamental abilities for humans, but developing machines capable of performing these tasks remains highly complex. However, computational tasks that take the human brain over a year to complete can be solved by machines in under a minute. Therefore, artificial intelligence focuses on how tasks that are effortless for humans can be implemented in artificially created machines so they can operate using acquired intelligence.

However, no machine has yet been developed that operates with 100% intelligence, and achieving this remains a highly complex task. Today, many machines employ some degree of artificial intelligence to carry out their functions. However, it is important to understand that artificial intelligence is not a branch of computational science. If a machine performs only calculations—though it may appear to act intelligently—we cannot call this intelligence, as there is no actual intelligence involved in the computation, which is a straightforward process. Therefore, for a machine to achieve artificial intelligence, it must have the capacity to make decisions based on heuristics, inferences, and fuzzy logic, and it must be able to learn from past experiences to make better decisions.
